The Sacrament of Baptism

The first and foundational sacrament of Christian initiation. Through water and the Word, we are freed from sin, reborn as children of God, and incorporated into the Body of Christ.

What Is Baptism?

Baptism is the gateway to the sacraments and to the life of grace. It is called a sacrament of initiation because it is the door through which we enter the Church. Through Baptism, original sin is washed away, we receive sanctifying grace, and we become members of Christ's Body. It imprints an indelible spiritual character on the soul — which is why Baptism can never be received a second time.

Scriptural Foundation
John 3:5
"Truly, truly, I say to you, unless one is born of water and the Spirit, he cannot enter the kingdom of God."
Matthew 28:19
"Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit."
Romans 6:3–4
"Do you not know that all of us who have been baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into his death? We were buried therefore with him by baptism into death, in order that, just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, we too might walk in newness of life."
Acts 2:38
"Repent and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins, and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit."
From the Catechism

Holy Baptism is the basis of the whole Christian life, the gateway to life in the Spirit, and the door which gives access to the other sacraments. Through Baptism we are freed from sin and reborn as sons of God; we become members of Christ, are incorporated into the Church and made sharers in her mission.

CCC 1213
Wisdom of the Saints

"Baptism is God's most beautiful and magnificent gift. We call it gift, grace, anointing, enlightenment, garment of immortality, bath of rebirth, seal, and most precious gift. It is called gift because it is conferred on those who bring nothing of their own; grace since it is given even to the guilty; Baptism because sin is buried in the water."

— St. Gregory of Nazianzus
The Four Essential Elements
💧
Water
The matter of the sacrament — poured or by immersion — signifying the washing away of sin and new birth in the Spirit.
🕊️
The Trinitarian Formula
"I baptize you in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit." These words are absolutely essential to the validity of Baptism.
🕯️
Indelible Character
Baptism imprints an indelible spiritual mark on the soul — a character — that consecrates the person permanently for divine worship. This is why Baptism cannot be repeated.
⛪
Incorporation into the Church
Through Baptism we are incorporated into the Body of Christ, becoming members of the Church and heirs to the promise of eternal life.
🌊
Threefold Effects
Baptism forgives original sin and all personal sins, bestows sanctifying grace and the theological virtues, and makes us temples of the Holy Spirit.
👤
Who Can Baptize?
In ordinary circumstances, a bishop, priest, or deacon baptizes. In a case of necessity, any person — even a non-Christian — can baptize with water and the proper intention.

The Sacrament of Confirmation

The second sacrament of initiation. It perfects Baptismal grace and seals the Christian with the fullness of the Holy Spirit, strengthening them to live and defend the faith.

What Is Confirmation?

Confirmation completes the grace of Baptism. By it, the baptized are more perfectly bound to the Church and are enriched with a special strength of the Holy Spirit. They are, as true witnesses of Christ, more strictly obliged to spread and defend the faith. The name "Confirmation" suggests that this sacrament both confirms and strengthens Baptismal grace. Like Baptism, Confirmation imprints an indelible spiritual mark on the soul and may be received only once.

Scriptural Foundation
Acts 8:14–17
"Now when the apostles at Jerusalem heard that Samaria had received the word of God, they sent to them Peter and John, who came down and prayed for them that they might receive the Holy Spirit... Then they laid their hands on them and they received the Holy Spirit."
Acts 2:1–4
"When the day of Pentecost arrived, they were all together in one place. And suddenly there came from heaven a sound like a mighty rushing wind, and it filled the entire house where they were sitting... And they were all filled with the Holy Spirit."
Isaiah 11:2–3
"The Spirit of the LORD shall rest upon him, the Spirit of wisdom and understanding, the Spirit of counsel and might, the Spirit of knowledge and the fear of the LORD."
From the Catechism

Confirmation brings an increase and deepening of baptismal grace: it roots us more deeply in the divine filiation; it unites us more firmly to Christ; it increases the gifts of the Holy Spirit in us; it renders our bond with the Church more perfect; it gives us a special strength of the Holy Spirit to spread and defend the faith by word and action as true witnesses of Christ, to confess the name of Christ boldly, and never to be ashamed of the Cross.

CCC 1303
Wisdom of the Saints

"Remember that you have received the spiritual seal, the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of right judgment and courage, the spirit of knowledge and reverence, the spirit of holy fear in God's presence. Guard what you have received. God the Father has marked you with his sign; Christ the Lord has confirmed you and has placed his pledge, the Spirit, in your hearts."

— St. Ambrose of Milan
The Seven Gifts of the Holy Spirit
📖
Wisdom
Enables us to see life from God's perspective and to judge all things according to divine truth.
💡
Understanding
Deepens our grasp of the mysteries of the faith so that we may penetrate their meaning.
🧭
Counsel (Right Judgment)
Guides us to make good decisions in accordance with God's will, especially in complex moral situations.
⚡
Fortitude (Courage)
Strengthens us to overcome fear and to stand firm in the faith, even at the cost of sacrifice.
🔑
Knowledge
Allows us to evaluate created things rightly and to see their relationship to God.
🙏
Piety (Reverence)
Moves us to give loving worship to God and to treat others with the reverence due to children of God.
😇
Fear of the Lord
Not servile fear, but a holy awe of God's majesty and a deep unwillingness to offend him who is all-good.
The Essentials of the Rite
✝️
The Anointing
The essential rite: anointing the forehead with sacred chrism with the laying on of the bishop's hand and the words: "Be sealed with the Gift of the Holy Spirit."
🤝
The Sponsor
The sponsor (distinct from the Baptismal godparent, though the same person may serve) helps the confirmed live as a faithful witness of Christ.
✏️
The Confirmation Name
Choosing a saint's name expresses one's desire to imitate that saint and to have them as an intercessor before God.
👨‍⚕️
The Minister
Ordinarily the bishop confirms, as a sign of the bond with the whole Church. A priest may confirm adults at Easter Vigil or in cases of necessity.

The Holy Eucharist

The source and summit of the Christian life. In the Eucharist, Christ gives us himself — his Body, Blood, Soul, and Divinity — truly present under the appearances of bread and wine.

What Is the Eucharist?

The Eucharist is the third and culminating sacrament of Christian initiation and the ongoing center of the Church's life. At every Mass, through the power of the Holy Spirit and the words of consecration, the bread and wine become the Body and Blood of Jesus Christ. This change — called transubstantiation — is total and real. Christ is not symbolically present; he is truly, substantially, and permanently present in the Blessed Sacrament. The Eucharist is simultaneously a sacrifice, a memorial, and a sacred meal in which we receive the Bread of Life.

Scriptural Foundation
John 6:51
"I am the living bread that came down from heaven. If anyone eats of this bread, he will live forever. And the bread that I will give for the life of the world is my flesh."
John 6:54–56
"Whoever feeds on my flesh and drinks my blood has eternal life, and I will raise him up on the last day. For my flesh is true food, and my blood is true drink."
Luke 22:19–20
"And he took bread, and when he had given thanks, he broke it and gave it to them, saying, "This is my body, which is given for you. Do this in remembrance of me.""
1 Corinthians 11:27
"Whoever, therefore, eats the bread or drinks the cup of the Lord in an unworthy manner will be guilty concerning the body and blood of the Lord."
From the Catechism

The Eucharist is "the source and summit of the Christian life." The other sacraments, and indeed all ecclesiastical ministries and works of the apostolate, are bound up with the Eucharist and are oriented toward it. For in the blessed Eucharist is contained the whole spiritual good of the Church, namely Christ himself, our Pasch.

CCC 1324
Wisdom of the Saints

"Jesus has made himself the Bread of Life to give us life. Night and day, He is there. If you really want to grow in love, come back to the Eucharist, come back to that Adoration."

— St. Teresa of Calcutta
Key Doctrines
🍞
Real Presence
Christ is truly present — Body, Blood, Soul, and Divinity — in the Eucharist. This is not a symbol, metaphor, or memory.
⚗️
Transubstantiation
The substance of the bread and wine is entirely changed into the Body and Blood of Christ at the consecration, though the appearances remain.
🛐
Sacrifice of the Mass
The Mass is not a re-sacrifice but a re-presentation of Christ's one, perfect sacrifice on Calvary, made present across time.
🤲
Holy Communion
Receiving the Eucharist in a state of grace unites us intimately with Christ and with the whole Church — the Mystical Body.
⚠️
Proper Disposition
One must be free from mortal sin, having made a prior sacramental confession if needed, and observing the Eucharistic fast.
🏛️
Perpetual Adoration
The Real Presence continues after Mass in the tabernacle, inviting us to prayer, adoration, and contemplation before the Lord.
Preparing for First Holy Communion

Before receiving Holy Communion for the first time, OCIA candidates must understand what the Church teaches about the Real Presence, be in a state of grace (free from mortal sin), have made a good First Confession, observe the one-hour Eucharistic fast before Mass, and approach with reverence and faith. The Eucharist is the greatest gift we can receive — Christ himself. Approach it with awe, gratitude, and love.

The Sacrament of Reconciliation

One of the two sacraments of healing. Through the ministry of the priest, God forgives sins committed after Baptism and reconciles the sinner with himself and with the Church.

What Is Reconciliation?

After Baptism, we can still sin and fall away from God's grace. The Sacrament of Reconciliation (also called Penance or Confession) is the ordinary means by which the baptized have their post-baptismal sins forgiven. Christ himself entrusted this power to his apostles: "Receive the Holy Spirit. If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven them" (John 20:22–23). Through sincere confession to a priest and sacramental absolution, God grants pardon, peace, and the restoration of friendship with him. The sacrament also heals wounds in the soul caused by sin and grants strength to avoid future sin.

Scriptural Foundation
John 20:22–23
"He breathed on them and said to them, "Receive the Holy Spirit. If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven them; if you withhold forgiveness from any, it is withheld.""
2 Corinthians 5:18
"All this is from God, who through Christ reconciled us to himself and gave us the ministry of reconciliation."
Luke 15:20
"But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and felt compassion, and ran and embraced him and kissed him. — The Parable of the Prodigal Son"
James 5:16
"Therefore, confess your sins to one another and pray for one another, that you may be healed."
From the Catechism

Those who approach the sacrament of Penance obtain pardon from God's mercy for the offense committed against him, and are, at the same time, reconciled with the Church which they have wounded by their sins and which by charity, by example, and by prayer labors for their conversion.

CCC 1422
Wisdom of the Saints

"Go to confession frequently. Confession is an act of honesty and courage — an act of entrusting ourselves, beyond sin, to the mercy of a loving and forgiving God. It is an act of the prodigal son who returns to his Father, who waits for us always."

— St. John Paul II
The Five Acts of the Penitent
🔍
1. Examination of Conscience
Prayerfully review your thoughts, words, deeds, and omissions since your last confession, guided by the Ten Commandments and the Beatitudes.
💔
2. Contrition
Genuine sorrow for sins — motivated not only by fear of punishment, but by love of God whom we have offended. Perfect contrition arises from love of God above all.
🤐
3. Resolution Not to Sin Again
A firm purpose of amendment: the sincere intention to avoid sin and its near occasions, relying on God's grace. Without this, the sacrament is not valid.
🗣️
4. Confession of Sins
Tell all mortal sins (kind and number) to the priest. Venial sins should also be confessed. The seal of confession is absolute and inviolable.
✅
5. Penance and Satisfaction
Accept and fulfill the penance given by the priest as a sign of love, reparation for sin, and amendment of life.
🔓
Sacramental Absolution
The priest, acting in the person of Christ, pronounces the words of absolution: "I absolve you from your sins in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit."

Anointing of the Sick

The second sacrament of healing. Through anointing with sacred oil and prayer, Christ strengthens, comforts, and sometimes heals those who are seriously ill, suffering, or near death.

What Is the Anointing of the Sick?

This sacrament is not reserved for those at the moment of death — though it may certainly be administered then (known as Viaticum when given with the Eucharist). It is for any Catholic who is seriously ill, facing major surgery, or weakened by old age. The sacrament unites the suffering of the sick with the Passion of Christ, brings comfort and courage to the soul, forgives sins if the sick person cannot confess, and may restore physical health if that is God's will. A priest anoints the sick person's forehead and hands with the Oil of the Sick, which has been blessed by a bishop.

Scriptural Foundation
James 5:14–15
"Is anyone among you sick? Let him call for the elders of the church, and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord. And the prayer of faith will save the one who is sick, and the Lord will raise him up. And if he has committed sins, he will be forgiven."
Mark 6:13
"And they cast out many demons and anointed with oil many who were sick and healed them."
Matthew 25:36
"I was sick and you visited me... Truly, I say to you, as you did it to one of the least of these my brothers, you did it to me."
From the Catechism

"Is anyone among you sick? He should summon the presbyters of the church, and they should pray over him and anoint him with oil in the name of the Lord." The Church believes and confesses that among the seven sacraments there is one especially intended to strengthen those who are being tried by illness: the Anointing of the Sick.

CCC 1511
Wisdom of the Saints

"Union with the Passion of Christ. By the grace of this sacrament the sick person receives the strength and the gift of uniting himself more closely to Christ's Passion: in a certain way he is consecrated to bear fruit by configuration to the Savior's redemptive Passion."

— Catechism of the Catholic Church (CCC 1521)
The Gifts of This Sacrament
💪
Strength and Courage
The Holy Spirit renews trust in God and provides the strength to bear suffering, resist temptation, and face death with peace.
🕊️
Union with Christ's Passion
The sick person's suffering, united to Christ's own, becomes a sharing in his redemptive work for the salvation of souls.
🙏
Forgiveness of Sins
If the sick person cannot receive Reconciliation, this sacrament forgives sins and removes the remnants of sin from the soul.
🏥
Healing
Sometimes God restores bodily health through this sacrament, if such is conducive to the salvation of the soul.
👨‍👩‍👧
Who Can Receive It?
Any Catholic in danger of death from illness or old age. It may be received multiple times during the same illness if the condition worsens.
👨‍🍳
Who Administers It?
Only a validly ordained priest can administer the Anointing of the Sick.

The Sacrament of Holy Orders

One of the two sacraments at the service of communion. Through Holy Orders, Christ continues to act in and through his Church by consecrating men as deacons, priests, and bishops.

What Is Holy Orders?

Holy Orders is the sacrament through which the mission entrusted by Christ to his apostles continues to be exercised in the Church until the end of time. It is the sacrament of apostolic ministry. The ordained minister acts not in his own name but "in the person of Christ the Head" (in persona Christi Capitis), which means that it is truly Christ who acts through the ordained man in the sacraments. Holy Orders has three degrees: episcopate (bishops), presbyterate (priests), and diaconate (deacons). Like Baptism and Confirmation, Holy Orders imprints an indelible character on the soul.

Scriptural Foundation
John 20:21–23
"Jesus said to them again, "Peace be with you. As the Father has sent me, even so I am sending you." And when he had said this, he breathed on them and said to them, "Receive the Holy Spirit. If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven them.""
Luke 22:19
"And he took bread, and when he had given thanks, he broke it and gave it to them, saying, "This is my body, which is given for you. Do this in remembrance of me.""
1 Timothy 4:14
"Do not neglect the gift you have, which was given you by prophecy when the council of elders laid their hands on you."
Hebrews 5:1
"For every high priest chosen from among men is appointed to act on behalf of men in relation to God, to offer gifts and sacrifices for sins."
From the Catechism

Holy Orders is the sacrament through which the mission entrusted by Christ to his apostles continues to be exercised in the Church until the end of time: thus it is the sacrament of apostolic ministry. It includes three degrees: episcopate, presbyterate, and diaconate.

CCC 1536
Wisdom of the Saints

"The priesthood is the love of the heart of Jesus. When you see a priest, think of our Lord Jesus Christ."

— St. John Vianney, Patron of Parish Priests
The Three Degrees of Holy Orders
👑
The Episcopate (Bishops)
The fullness of the sacrament of Holy Orders. Bishops are successors of the Apostles, responsible for teaching, sanctifying, and governing a particular Church (diocese).
✝️
The Presbyterate (Priests)
Priests collaborate with their bishop and act in the person of Christ the Head. They celebrate the Eucharist, forgive sins, anoint the sick, preach, and shepherd their parishes.
🤲
The Diaconate (Deacons)
Deacons are ordained for service: assisting at the Eucharist, baptizing, witnessing marriages, proclaiming the Gospel, and serving the poor. Permanent deacons may be married.
🔏
Indelible Character
Like Baptism and Confirmation, Holy Orders imprints an indelible mark on the soul. A priest is a priest forever, even if removed from active ministry.
👨
Male Ordinands Only
The Church teaches that, following the example of Christ who chose only male apostles, only baptized men can validly receive Holy Orders (CCC 1577).
🙌
Apostolic Succession
Every validly ordained bishop can trace his ordination in an unbroken line back to the Apostles, ensuring the continuity of Christ's mission in the Church.

The Sacrament of Matrimony

The second sacrament at the service of communion. Christian marriage is a covenant and a sacrament — an image of Christ's faithful, fruitful, and indissoluble love for his Church.

What Is Christian Marriage?

The matrimonial covenant, by which a man and a woman establish between themselves a partnership of the whole of life, is by its nature ordered toward the good of the spouses and the procreation and education of children. Christ raised this natural institution to the dignity of a sacrament. In the Latin Church, the spouses themselves are the ministers of the sacrament — they confer the sacrament upon each other by exchanging vows. The marriage of two baptized Catholics is, by that very fact, a sacrament. Three essential properties define Christian marriage: unity (one man, one woman), indissolubility (for life), and openness to children.

Scriptural Foundation
Genesis 2:24
"Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his wife, and they shall become one flesh."
Matthew 19:6
"So they are no longer two but one flesh. What therefore God has joined together, let not man separate."
Ephesians 5:31–32
""Therefore a man shall leave his father and mother and hold fast to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh." This mystery is profound, and I am saying that it refers to Christ and the church."
John 2:1–2
"On the third day there was a wedding at Cana in Galilee, and the mother of Jesus was there. Jesus also was invited to the wedding with his disciples. — Christ honored marriage with his presence and first miracle."
From the Catechism

The matrimonial covenant, by which a man and a woman establish between themselves a partnership of the whole of life, is by its nature ordered toward the good of the spouses and the procreation and education of offspring; this covenant between baptized persons has been raised by Christ the Lord to the dignity of a sacrament.

CCC 1601
Wisdom of the Saints

"To love is not to look at one another, but to look together in the same direction. Marriage is not two people facing each other, but two people standing side by side, walking together toward God."

— Antoine de Saint-Exupéry (reflecting Franciscan wisdom on vocation)
The Properties of Christian Marriage
💑
Unity
Marriage is between one man and one woman — a communion of persons reflecting the unity of the Trinity and of Christ with the Church.
♾️
Indissolubility
What God has joined, no human authority can put asunder. A valid, consummated sacramental marriage cannot be dissolved.
👶
Openness to Life
Spouses are called to be co-creators with God. The deliberate exclusion of children is contrary to the nature of the marriage covenant.
🤝
Mutual Consent
Free, full, faithful, and fruitful consent makes the marriage. Force or grave fear invalidates consent.
📿
Domestic Church
The Christian family is called the "domestic church" — the first place where children learn faith, prayer, and love.
🙏
Grace for the Journey
The sacrament gives spouses a special grace to love as Christ loves — sacrificially, perseveringly, and with supernatural charity.

Sacred Scripture

God speaks to us through his holy Word. Scripture and Tradition together form the one sacred deposit of the Word of God.

Why Scripture Matters

Sacred Scripture is the speech of God as it is put down in writing under the breath of the Holy Spirit (CCC 81). The Church venerates Scripture as she venerates the Body of the Lord himself. Scripture does not stand alone — it is always interpreted within the living Tradition of the Church and under the authority of the Magisterium. For Catholics, faith comes through hearing (Romans 10:17), and hearing the Word of God proclaimed at Mass is an encounter with the living Christ.

The Great Commission
Matthew 28:18–20

Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you. Romans 8:14–15
"For all who are led by the Spirit of God are sons of God. For you did not receive the spirit of slavery to fall back into fear, but you have received the Spirit of adoption as sons, by whom we cry, "Abba! Father!""

The Rosary is prayed with a set of beads. Begin at the crucifix and work around the beads through five decades (groups of ten), each dedicated to a mystery of Christ's life. The Rosary is a meditation — the vocal prayers are the backdrop for contemplating the mysteries.

  1. Make the Sign of the Cross and pray the Apostles' Creed on the crucifix.
  2. Pray one Our Father, three Hail Marys (for faith, hope, and charity), and one Glory Be on the introductory beads.
  3. Announce the first mystery, then pray one Our Father.
  4. Pray ten Hail Marys while meditating on the mystery.
  5. Pray one Glory Be and the Fatima Prayer: "O my Jesus, forgive us our sins, save us from the fires of hell, lead all souls to heaven, especially those in most need of Thy mercy."
  6. Repeat steps 3–5 for the remaining four decades.
  7. Conclude with the Hail Holy Queen (Salve Regina).

Given by Our Lord to St. Faustina Kowalska, this chaplet is prayed on ordinary Rosary beads and is especially powerful at 3:00 PM — the Hour of Mercy.

  1. Begin with an optional opening prayer: "You expired, Jesus, but the source of life gushed forth for souls…"
  2. Pray the Our Father, Hail Mary, and Apostles' Creed.
  3. On the Our Father beads (5 times): "Eternal Father, I offer You the Body and Blood, Soul and Divinity of Your dearly beloved Son, Our Lord Jesus Christ, in atonement for our sins and those of the whole world."
  4. On the Hail Mary beads (10 times per decade): "For the sake of His sorrowful Passion, have mercy on us and on the whole world."
  5. After five decades, say three times: "Holy God, Holy Mighty One, Holy Immortal One, have mercy on us and on the whole world."
  6. Close with the optional closing prayer.

Revealed to a Carmelite nun in the 18th century, this chaplet honors the nine choirs of angels and invokes St. Michael's protection. It consists of nine salutations, each followed by one Our Father and three Hail Marys.

  1. Begin: "O God, come to my assistance. O Lord, make haste to help me."
  2. For each of the nine salutations (honoring the Seraphim, Cherubim, Thrones, Dominions, Virtues, Powers, Principalities, Archangels, and Angels), pray one Our Father and three Hail Marys.
  3. After the nine salutations, pray four Our Fathers in honor of St. Michael, St. Gabriel, St. Raphael, and your Guardian Angel.
  4. Conclude with the prayer: "O glorious prince St. Michael, chief and commander of the heavenly hosts..."

The Franciscan Crown is a special Marian rosary given to a young Franciscan by Our Lady herself. It consists of seven decades — one for each of the seven joys of Mary — making it particularly fitting for our Franciscan parish family.

  1. Begin with an Our Father and two Hail Marys.
  2. For each of the seven decades, announce the Joy of Mary, pray one Our Father, ten Hail Marys, and one Glory Be.
  3. The Seven Joys: 1. The Annunciation · 2. The Visitation · 3. The Nativity · 4. The Adoration of the Magi · 5. The Finding in the Temple · 6. The Resurrection · 7. The Assumption and Coronation.
  4. At the end, pray two additional Hail Marys (totaling 72, honoring the years of Our Lady's earthly life according to tradition).
  5. Conclude with the Sign of the Cross and a brief prayer of thanksgiving.

The Chaplet of St. Joseph honors the earthly father of Jesus and patron of the universal Church. It is prayed on seven groups of beads, recalling the seven sorrows and joys of St. Joseph.

  1. Begin with the Sign of the Cross and an opening prayer to St. Joseph.
  2. For each of the seven groups, pray one Our Father, one Hail Mary, and the ejaculation: "Blessed be St. Joseph, her most chaste spouse."
  3. Conclude with three Hail Marys for the intentions of the Holy Father.
  4. Close: "To you, O Blessed Joseph, we have recourse in our affliction..."

This chaplet is especially fitting for OCIA candidates who are preparing to receive the Holy Spirit in Confirmation. It consists of five groups of seven beads, honoring the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit.

  1. Begin with an act of consecration to the Holy Spirit.
  2. For each of the five groups of seven beads, pray the Come Holy Spirit prayer seven times.
  3. Meditate on one Gift of the Holy Spirit per group: Wisdom · Understanding · Counsel · Fortitude · Knowledge · Piety · Fear of the Lord.
  4. Conclude with: "Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of your faithful, and kindle in them the fire of your love."

This chaplet meditates on the love of Christ's Sacred Heart and was promoted by St. Margaret Mary Alacoque. It consists of thirty-three beads honoring each year of Christ's life.

  1. Begin with the Sign of the Cross and: "O Lord Jesus Christ, to your Sacred Heart I consecrate and offer up my person and my life."
  2. Pray one Our Father and one Hail Mary.
  3. Then pray the following 33 times on each of the 33 beads: "Sweet Heart of Jesus, be my love."
  4. Conclude: "Sweet Heart of Mary, be my salvation."

The Communion of Saints

The saints are our brothers and sisters in Christ who have been perfected in God's love. They intercede for us, inspire us, and show us the way to heaven.

What Is the Communion of Saints?

The Communion of Saints refers to the spiritual union of all the faithful — the Church Militant (pilgrims on earth), the Church Suffering (souls in Purgatory), and the Church Triumphant (the blessed in Heaven). We are not alone on our journey. The saints in Heaven pray for us unceasingly, and we can ask for their intercession just as we ask friends on earth to pray for us. Asking a saint to pray is not worship — that belongs to God alone — but it is a powerful communion in the Body of Christ that transcends death.

St. Francis of Assisi — Patron of Our Parish Family

"Begin by doing what is necessary; then do what is possible; and suddenly you are doing the impossible. Lord, make me an instrument of your peace: where there is hatred, let me sow love; where there is injury, pardon; where there is doubt, faith; where there is despair, hope; where there is darkness, light; where there is sadness, joy."

Patron Saints for OCIA Candidates
🦁
St. Augustine of Hippo
Convert, theologian, and Doctor of the Church. Patron of those seeking God after years away from faith. His Confessions remain the greatest spiritual autobiography ever written.
🌹
St. Thérèse of Lisieux
The Little Flower, Doctor of the Church. Her "little way" of spiritual childhood — doing small things with great love — is perfect for those new to the faith.
🌊
St. Mary Magdalene
Apostle to the Apostles. The first witness of the Resurrection. Patron of those who seek Jesus and those returning to the faith with renewed hearts.
⚓
St. Peter the Apostle
The first Pope, who denied Christ and was restored by his mercy. Patron of those who have doubted or wandered, a witness that no fall is final with God.
✝️
St. Paul the Apostle
Greatest convert in history. Former persecutor of Christians who became the Apostle to the Gentiles. Patron of OCIA candidates and all who undergo radical conversion.
🌿
St. Clare of Assisi
Co-founder of the Poor Clares, companion of St. Francis, and model of Franciscan poverty and contemplation. Patron of television and those who seek simplicity.
A Prayer to Your Patron Saint

O [Name of Saint], through your life of faith, sacrifice, and love, you followed Christ with courage and joy. I ask for your intercession and the grace to imitate your virtues. Pray for me before the throne of God, that I may persevere in faith, receive the sacraments with an open heart, and one day join you in eternal life. Amen.
