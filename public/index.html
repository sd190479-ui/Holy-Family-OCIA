What is OCIA?

The Order of Christian Initiation of Adults is the sacred process by which adults enter the Catholic Church. Rooted in the practice of the early Church, it is not a class to complete — it is a journey of conversion, accompanied by the parish community, guided by the Holy Spirit, and culminating in the Sacraments of Baptism, Confirmation, and the Holy Eucharist at the Easter Vigil. Here at Holy Family, this journey unfolds in the spirit of St. Francis of Assisi — with simplicity, joy, and a profound reverence for the presence of God in all things.

The Sacrament of Baptism

The first and foundational sacrament of Christian initiation. Through water and the Word, we are freed from sin, reborn as children of God, and incorporated into the Body of Christ.

What Is Baptism?

Baptism is the gateway to the sacraments and to the life of grace. It is called a sacrament of initiation because it is the door through which we enter the Church. Through Baptism, original sin is washed away, we receive sanctifying grace, and we become members of Christ's Body. It imprints an indelible spiritual character on the soul — which is why Baptism can never be received a second time.

Scriptural Foundation
John 3:5
“Truly, truly, I say to you, unless one is born of water and the Spirit, he cannot enter the kingdom of God.”
Matthew 28:19
“Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.”
Romans 6:3–4
“Do you not know that all of us who have been baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into his death? We were buried with him by baptism into death, so that we too might walk in newness of life.”
Acts 2:38
“Repent and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins, and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.”
From the Catechism

Holy Baptism is the basis of the whole Christian life, the gateway to life in the Spirit, and the door which gives access to the other sacraments. Through Baptism we are freed from sin and reborn as sons of God; we become members of Christ, are incorporated into the Church and made sharers in her mission.

CCC 1213
Wisdom of the Saints

“Baptism is God's most beautiful and magnificent gift. We call it gift, grace, anointing, enlightenment, garment of immortality, bath of rebirth, seal, and most precious gift.”

— St. Gregory of Nazianzus
The Four Essential Elements
💧
Water
The matter of the sacrament — poured or by immersion — signifying the washing away of sin and new birth in the Spirit.
🕊️
The Trinitarian Formula
"I baptize you in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit." These words are absolutely essential to the validity of Baptism.
🕯️
Indelible Character
Baptism imprints an indelible spiritual mark on the soul — a character — that consecrates the person permanently for divine worship. This is why Baptism cannot be repeated.
⛪
Incorporation into the Church
Through Baptism we are incorporated into the Body of Christ, becoming members of the Church and heirs to the promise of eternal life.
🌊
Threefold Effects
Baptism forgives original sin and all personal sins, bestows sanctifying grace and the theological virtues, and makes us temples of the Holy Spirit.
👤
Who Can Baptize?
In ordinary circumstances, a bishop, priest, or deacon baptizes. In a case of necessity, any person — even a non-Christian — can baptize with water and the proper intention.
🔥

The Sacrament of Confirmation

The second sacrament of initiation. It perfects Baptismal grace and seals the Christian with the fullness of the Holy Spirit, strengthening them to live and defend the faith.

What Is Confirmation?

Confirmation completes the grace of Baptism. By it, the baptized are more perfectly bound to the Church and are enriched with a special strength of the Holy Spirit. They are, as true witnesses of Christ, more strictly obliged to spread and defend the faith. Like Baptism, Confirmation imprints an indelible spiritual mark on the soul and may be received only once.

Scriptural Foundation
Acts 8:14–17
“Now when the apostles at Jerusalem heard that Samaria had received the word of God, they sent to them Peter and John, who came down and prayed for them that they might receive the Holy Spirit... Then they laid their hands on them and they received the Holy Spirit.”
Acts 2:1–4
“When the day of Pentecost arrived, they were all together in one place. And suddenly there came from heaven a sound like a mighty rushing wind... And they were all filled with the Holy Spirit.”
Isaiah 11:2–3
“The Spirit of the LORD shall rest upon him, the Spirit of wisdom and understanding, the Spirit of counsel and might, the Spirit of knowledge and the fear of the LORD.”
From the Catechism

Confirmation brings an increase and deepening of baptismal grace: it roots us more deeply in the divine filiation; it unites us more firmly to Christ; it increases the gifts of the Holy Spirit in us; it renders our bond with the Church more perfect; it gives us a special strength of the Holy Spirit to spread and defend the faith by word and action as true witnesses of Christ.

CCC 1303
Wisdom of the Saints

“Remember that you have received the spiritual seal, the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of right judgment and courage, the spirit of knowledge and reverence, the spirit of holy fear in God's presence. Guard what you have received.”

— St. Ambrose of Milan
The Seven Gifts of the Holy Spirit
📖
Wisdom
Enables us to see life from God's perspective and to judge all things according to divine truth.
💡
Understanding
Deepens our grasp of the mysteries of the faith so that we may penetrate their meaning.
🧭
Counsel (Right Judgment)
Guides us to make good decisions in accordance with God's will, especially in complex moral situations.
⚡
Fortitude (Courage)
Strengthens us to overcome fear and to stand firm in the faith, even at the cost of sacrifice.
🔑
Knowledge
Allows us to evaluate created things rightly and to see their relationship to God.
🙏
Piety (Reverence)
Moves us to give loving worship to God and to treat others with the reverence due to children of God.
😇
Fear of the Lord
Not servile fear, but a holy awe of God's majesty and a deep unwillingness to offend him who is all-good.
The Essentials of the Rite
✝️
The Anointing
The essential rite: anointing the forehead with sacred chrism with the laying on of the bishop's hand and the words: "Be sealed with the Gift of the Holy Spirit."
🤝
The Sponsor
The sponsor helps the confirmed live as a faithful witness of Christ. They may be the same as the baptismal godparent.
✏️
The Confirmation Name
Choosing a saint's name expresses one's desire to imitate that saint and to have them as an intercessor before God.
👨‍⚕️
The Minister
Ordinarily the bishop confirms, as a sign of the bond with the whole Church. A priest may confirm adults at Easter Vigil.
🍞

The Holy Eucharist

The source and summit of the Christian life. In the Eucharist, Christ gives us himself — truly present under the appearances of bread and wine.

What Is the Eucharist?

The Eucharist is the third and culminating sacrament of Christian initiation. At every Mass, through the power of the Holy Spirit and the words of consecration, the bread and wine become the Body and Blood of Jesus Christ. This change — called transubstantiation — is total and real. Christ is not symbolically present; he is truly, substantially, and permanently present in the Blessed Sacrament.

Scriptural Foundation
John 6:51
"I am the living bread that came down from heaven. If anyone eats of this bread, he will live forever."
Luke 22:19–20
"And he took bread, and when he had given thanks, he broke it and gave it to them, saying, "This is my body, which is given for you. Do this in remembrance of me.""
1 Corinthians 11:27
"Whoever, therefore, eats the bread or drinks the cup of the Lord in an unworthy manner will be guilty concerning the body and blood of the Lord."
From the Catechism

The Eucharist is "the source and summit of the Christian life." For in the blessed Eucharist is contained the whole spiritual good of the Church, namely Christ himself, our Pasch.

CCC 1324
Wisdom of the Saints

"Jesus has made himself the Bread of Life to give us life. Night and day, He is there. If you really want to grow in love, come back to the Eucharist."

— St. Teresa of Calcutta / Santa Teresa de Calcuta
Key Doctrines
🍞
Real Presence
Christ is truly present — Body, Blood, Soul, and Divinity — in the Eucharist. This is not a symbol, metaphor, or memory.
⚗️
Transubstantiation
The substance of the bread and wine is entirely changed into the Body and Blood of Christ at the consecration, though the appearances remain.
🛐
Sacrifice of the Mass
The Mass is a re-presentation of Christ's one, perfect sacrifice on Calvary, made present across time.
🤲
Holy Communion
Receiving the Eucharist in a state of grace unites us intimately with Christ and with the whole Church — the Mystical Body.
⚠️
Proper Disposition
One must be free from mortal sin, having made a prior sacramental confession if needed, and observing the Eucharistic fast.
🏛️
Adoration
The Real Presence continues after Mass in the tabernacle, inviting us to prayer, adoration, and contemplation before the Lord.
🕊️

The Sacrament of Reconciliation

One of the two sacraments of healing. Through the ministry of the priest, God forgives sins committed after Baptism.

What Is Reconciliation?

After Baptism, we can still sin and fall away from God's grace. The Sacrament of Reconciliation is the ordinary means by which the baptized have their post-baptismal sins forgiven. Christ himself entrusted this power to his apostles: "If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven them." Through sincere confession and sacramental absolution, God grants pardon, peace, and the restoration of friendship with him.

Scriptural Foundation
John 20:22–23
"He breathed on them and said, "Receive the Holy Spirit. If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven them; if you withhold forgiveness from any, it is withheld.""
Luke 15:20
"But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and felt compassion, and ran and embraced him and kissed him. — The Parable of the Prodigal Son"
From the Catechism

Those who approach the sacrament of Penance obtain pardon from God's mercy for the offense committed against him, and are, at the same time, reconciled with the Church.

CCC 1422
Wisdom of the Saints

"Go to confession frequently. Confession is an act of honesty and courage — an act of entrusting ourselves to the mercy of a loving and forgiving God."

— St. John Paul II / San Juan Pablo II
The Five Acts of the Penitent
🔍
1. Examination of Conscience
Prayerfully review your thoughts, words, deeds, and omissions since your last confession.
💔
2. Contrition
Genuine sorrow for sins motivated not only by fear, but by love of God whom we have offended.
🤐
3. Resolution Not to Sin Again
A firm intention to avoid sin and its near occasions, relying on God's grace.
🗣️
4. Confession of Sins
Tell all mortal sins (kind and number) to the priest. The seal of confession is absolute and inviolable.
✅
5. Penance
Accept and fulfill the penance given by the priest as a sign of love and reparation for sin.
🔓
Sacramental Absolution
The priest, acting in the person of Christ, pronounces the words of absolution.
🫒

Anointing of the Sick

The second sacrament of healing. Through anointing with sacred oil and prayer, Christ strengthens and comforts those who are seriously ill.

What Is the Anointing of the Sick?

This sacrament is not reserved for those at the moment of death. It is for any Catholic who is seriously ill, facing major surgery, or weakened by old age. The sacrament unites the suffering of the sick with the Passion of Christ, brings comfort and courage to the soul, forgives sins, and may restore physical health if that is God's will.

Scriptural Foundation
James 5:14–15
"Is anyone among you sick? Let him call for the elders of the church, and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord. And the prayer of faith will save the one who is sick."
From the Catechism

The Church believes and confesses that among the seven sacraments there is one especially intended to strengthen those who are being tried by illness: the Anointing of the Sick.

CCC 1511
The Gifts of This Sacrament
💪
Strength and Courage
The Holy Spirit renews trust in God and provides the strength to bear suffering and face death with peace.
🕊️
Union with Christ's Passion
The sick person's suffering, united to Christ's own, becomes a sharing in his redemptive work.
🙏
Forgiveness of Sins
If the sick person cannot receive Reconciliation, this sacrament forgives sins and removes their remnants.
🏥
Healing
Sometimes God restores bodily health through this sacrament, if such is conducive to the salvation of the soul.
👨‍👩‍👧
Who Can Receive It?
Any Catholic in danger of death from illness or old age. It may be received multiple times.
👨‍🍳
Who Administers It?
Only a validly ordained priest can administer the Anointing of the Sick.
⛪

The Sacrament of Holy Orders

One of the two sacraments at the service of communion. Through Holy Orders, Christ continues to act in and through his Church.

What Is Holy Orders?

Holy Orders is the sacrament through which the mission entrusted by Christ to his apostles continues to be exercised in the Church until the end of time. The ordained minister acts in the person of Christ the Head. Holy Orders has three degrees: episcopate (bishops), presbyterate (priests), and diaconate (deacons). Like Baptism and Confirmation, it imprints an indelible character on the soul.

Scriptural Foundation
John 20:21–23
"Jesus said to them again, "Peace be with you. As the Father has sent me, even so I am sending you." And when he had said this, he breathed on them and said, "Receive the Holy Spirit.""
Luke 22:19
"And he took bread, and when he had given thanks, he broke it and gave it to them, saying, "This is my body, which is given for you. Do this in remembrance of me.""
From the Catechism

Holy Orders is the sacrament through which the mission entrusted by Christ to his apostles continues to be exercised in the Church until the end of time: thus it is the sacrament of apostolic ministry. It includes three degrees: episcopate, presbyterate, and diaconate.

CCC 1536
Wisdom of the Saints

"The priesthood is the love of the heart of Jesus. When you see a priest, think of our Lord Jesus Christ."

— St. John Vianney / San Juan María Vianney
The Three Degrees of Holy Orders
👑
The Episcopate (Bishops)
The fullness of the sacrament of Holy Orders. Bishops are successors of the Apostles, responsible for teaching, sanctifying, and governing a diocese.
✝️
The Presbyterate (Priests)
Priests act in the person of Christ the Head. They celebrate the Eucharist, forgive sins, anoint the sick, preach, and shepherd their parishes.
🤲
The Diaconate (Deacons)
Deacons are ordained for service: assisting at the Eucharist, baptizing, witnessing marriages, and serving the poor. Permanent deacons may be married.
🔏
Indelible Character
Like Baptism and Confirmation, Holy Orders imprints an indelible mark. A priest is a priest forever.
👨
Male Ordinands Only
The Church teaches that, following Christ's example, only baptized men can validly receive Holy Orders (CCC 1577).
🙌
Apostolic Succession
Every validly ordained bishop can trace his ordination in an unbroken line back to the Apostles.
💍

The Sacrament of Matrimony

The second sacrament at the service of communion. Christian marriage is a covenant — an image of Christ's faithful, fruitful, and indissoluble love for his Church.

What Is Christian Marriage?

The matrimonial covenant, by which a man and a woman establish between themselves a partnership of the whole of life, is by its nature ordered toward the good of the spouses and the procreation and education of children. Christ raised this natural institution to the dignity of a sacrament. In the Latin Church, the spouses themselves are the ministers of the sacrament — they confer it upon each other by exchanging vows.

Scriptural Foundation
Genesis 2:24
"Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his wife, and they shall become one flesh."
Matthew 19:6
"So they are no longer two but one flesh. What therefore God has joined together, let not man separate."
Ephesians 5:31–32
""Therefore a man shall leave his father and mother and hold fast to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh." This mystery is profound, and I am saying that it refers to Christ and the church."
From the Catechism

The matrimonial covenant, by which a man and a woman establish between themselves a partnership of the whole of life, is by its nature ordered toward the good of the spouses and the procreation and education of offspring; this covenant between baptized persons has been raised by Christ the Lord to the dignity of a sacrament.

CCC 1601
The Properties of Christian Marriage
💑
Unity
Marriage is between one man and one woman — a communion of persons reflecting the unity of Christ with the Church.
♾️
Indissolubility
What God has joined, no human authority can put asunder. A valid, consummated sacramental marriage cannot be dissolved.
👶
Openness to Life
Spouses are called to be co-creators with God. The deliberate exclusion of children is contrary to the nature of the marriage covenant.
🤝
Mutual Consent
Free, full, faithful, and fruitful consent makes the marriage. Force or grave fear invalidates consent.
📿
Domestic Church
The Christian family is called the "domestic church" — the first place where children learn faith, prayer, and love.
🙏
Grace for the Journey
The sacrament gives spouses a special grace to love as Christ loves — sacrificially and with supernatural charity.

Sacred Scripture

God speaks to us through his holy Word. Scripture and Tradition together form the one sacred deposit of the Word of God.

Why Scripture Matters

Sacred Scripture is the speech of God as it is put down in writing under the breath of the Holy Spirit (CCC 81). The Church venerates Scripture as she venerates the Body of the Lord himself. Scripture does not stand alone — it is always interpreted within the living Tradition of the Church and under the authority of the Magisterium. For Catholics, faith comes through hearing (Romans 10:17), and hearing the Word of God proclaimed at Mass is an encounter with the living Christ.

    The Holy Rosary
    Guides & Instructions

    The Rosary is prayed with a set of beads. Begin at the crucifix and work around the beads through five decades (groups of ten), each dedicated to a mystery of Christ's life. The Rosary is a meditation — the vocal prayers are the backdrop for contemplating the mysteries.

    1. Make the Sign of the Cross and pray the Apostles' Creed on the crucifix.
    2. Pray one Our Father, three Hail Marys (for faith, hope, and charity), and one Glory Be on the introductory beads.
    3. Announce the first mystery, then pray one Our Father.
    4. Pray ten Hail Marys while meditating on the mystery.
    5. Pray one Glory Be and the Fatima Prayer.
    6. Repeat for the remaining four decades.
    7. Conclude with the Hail Holy Queen (Salve Regina).

    St. John Paul II added the Luminous Mysteries in 2002. The traditional schedule assigns a different set to each day of the week.

    1. Joyful Mysteries — Monday & Saturday: The Annunciation · Visitation · Nativity · Presentation · Finding in the Temple.
    2. Luminous Mysteries — Thursday: Baptism of Jesus · Wedding at Cana · Proclamation of the Kingdom · Transfiguration · Institution of the Eucharist.
    3. Sorrowful Mysteries — Tuesday & Friday: Agony in the Garden · Scourging · Crowning with Thorns · Carrying of the Cross · Crucifixion.
    4. Glorious Mysteries — Wednesday & Sunday: Resurrection · Ascension · Descent of the Holy Spirit · Assumption · Coronation of Mary.

    Given by Our Lord to St. Faustina Kowalska, this chaplet is especially powerful at 3:00 PM — the Hour of Mercy.

    1. Begin: Our Father, Hail Mary, Apostles' Creed.
    2. On the Our Father beads (5 times): "Eternal Father, I offer You the Body and Blood, Soul and Divinity of Your dearly beloved Son…"
    3. On the Hail Mary beads (10 × per decade): "For the sake of His sorrowful Passion, have mercy on us and on the whole world."
    4. Conclude three times: "Holy God, Holy Mighty One, Holy Immortal One, have mercy on us and on the whole world."

    The Franciscan Crown honors the Seven Joys of Mary with seven decades of Hail Marys — especially fitting for our Franciscan parish.

    1. Begin with an Our Father and two Hail Marys.
    2. For each of the seven decades: announce the Joy, pray one Our Father, ten Hail Marys, one Glory Be.
    3. The Seven Joys: Annunciation · Visitation · Nativity · Adoration of the Magi · Finding in the Temple · Resurrection · Assumption & Coronation.
    4. End with two additional Hail Marys (72 total, honoring Our Lady's years on earth).

    Nine salutations honor the nine choirs of angels. Each salutation is followed by one Our Father and three Hail Marys.

    1. Begin: "O God, come to my assistance. O Lord, make haste to help me."
    2. Pray the nine salutations (Seraphim, Cherubim, Thrones, Dominions, Virtues, Powers, Principalities, Archangels, Angels) — each with 1 Our Father + 3 Hail Marys.
    3. Pray four Our Fathers for St. Michael, St. Gabriel, St. Raphael, and your Guardian Angel.
    4. Conclude with the Prayer to St. Michael.

    The Communion of Saints

    The saints are our brothers and sisters in Christ who have been perfected in God's love. They intercede for us, inspire us, and show us the way to heaven.

    What Is the Communion of Saints?

    The Communion of Saints refers to the spiritual union of all the faithful — the Church Militant (pilgrims on earth), the Church Suffering (souls in Purgatory), and the Church Triumphant (the blessed in Heaven). We are not alone on our journey. The saints in Heaven pray for us unceasingly, and we can ask for their intercession just as we ask friends on earth to pray for us. Asking a saint to pray is not worship — that belongs to God alone — but it is a powerful communion in the Body of Christ that transcends death.

    St. Francis of Assisi — Patron of Our Parish Family

    "Begin by doing what is necessary; then do what is possible; and suddenly you are doing the impossible. Lord, make me an instrument of your peace: where there is hatred, let me sow love; where there is injury, pardon; where there is doubt, faith; where there is despair, hope; where there is darkness, light; where there is sadness, joy."

    — St. Francis of Assisi (1182–1226), Founder of the Franciscan Order

    The Liturgical Year

    The Church marks time differently. Every season, color, and feast draws us deeper into the mystery of Christ's life, death, and resurrection.

    Why the Church Has a Sacred Calendar

    The Catholic liturgical year is not a calendar of historical commemorations — it is a living participation in the mystery of Christ. Each year the Church re-enters and re-lives the saving events of Jesus: his coming, his birth, his ministry, his suffering, his resurrection, and the sending of his Spirit. The liturgical year begins not in January but on the First Sunday of Advent, four weeks before Christmas.

    The Seasons
    Advent
    4 Sundays before Christmas · Liturgical color: Purple/Rose
    A time of joyful, penitential waiting. We prepare for both Christ's first coming at Christmas and his second coming at the end of time. The Advent wreath marks the passing weeks with growing light.
    Christmas
    Christmas Day through the Baptism of the Lord · Color: White/Gold
    We celebrate the Incarnation — God becoming man. The season runs through the Feast of the Baptism of the Lord, the Sunday after Epiphany. White and gold symbolize the joy and purity of the Incarnate Word.
    Ordinary Time (First)
    After Baptism of the Lord until Ash Wednesday · Color: Green
    "Ordinary" comes from "ordinal" — the counted weeks. Green symbolizes hope and growth. We listen to Jesus teach, preach, and heal, growing in discipleship during these weeks of the year.
    Lent
    Ash Wednesday through Holy Thursday · Color: Purple
    Forty days of prayer, fasting, and almsgiving mirror Jesus' forty days in the desert. For OCIA candidates, this is the most intense period of preparation before Easter. The Three Scrutinies are celebrated during this season.
    Holy Week
    Palm Sunday through Holy Saturday · Colors: Red, then none
    The summit of the entire liturgical year. Palm Sunday recalls Jesus' entry into Jerusalem. The Easter Triduum — Holy Thursday, Good Friday, Holy Saturday — are three days counted as one continuous liturgy celebrating Christ's Passover from death to life.
    Easter
    Easter Sunday through Pentecost (50 days) · Color: White/Gold
    The Church's greatest and oldest feast. "Easter is not a day but a season," the Church teaches. The fifty days from Easter to Pentecost are celebrated as one great day of joy. For the newly baptized, this is the season of mystagogy — deeper exploration of the sacraments just received.
    Pentecost & Ordinary Time
    Pentecost Sunday through Advent · Colors: Red, then Green
    Pentecost Sunday closes the Easter season with the celebration of the Holy Spirit's descent on the Apostles. Ordinary Time then resumes for the remainder of the year — the long green season of growing in faith, guided by the Spirit given at Pentecost.
    Special Colors Explained
    Reference guide
    White/Gold — Joy, purity, glory (Christmas, Easter, feast days of Christ and Mary). Purple/Violet — Penance, preparation (Advent, Lent). Red — Fire of the Spirit, blood of martyrs (Pentecost, feasts of Apostles and martyrs). Green — Hope, growth (Ordinary Time). Rose — Joy amid penance (3rd Sunday of Advent, 4th Sunday of Lent). Black/Gold — Mourning (funerals).
    Key Feasts to Know
    🌟
    Solemnities
    The highest rank of feast day. Examples: Christmas, Easter, Pentecost, the Immaculate Conception (Dec 8), the Assumption (Aug 15), All Saints (Nov 1), and the feasts of the Lord throughout the year.
    📅
    Feast Days
    The second rank. Feasts of the Apostles, the major events of Mary's life, and other important celebrations. When a feast falls on a Sunday, the Sunday Mass normally takes priority.
    🕯️
    The Triduum
    Holy Thursday, Good Friday, and Holy Saturday are the Three Sacred Days (Triduum) — the very heart of the Christian year and the culmination of Lent. The Easter Vigil on Holy Saturday is the greatest night of the year.
    ✝️
    Holy Days of Obligation
    In the US: Christmas (Dec 25), Mary the Mother of God (Jan 1), Ascension Thursday, Assumption (Aug 15), All Saints (Nov 1), Immaculate Conception (Dec 8). Catholics are obliged to attend Mass on these days.

    The Mass Guide

    A step-by-step companion for every part of the Mass — what happens, what it means, and what to do. You are always welcome at Mass, even before you can receive Communion.

    Before You Arrive

    Arrive a few minutes early to settle and pray. You will see people genuflecting (briefly kneeling on one knee) toward the tabernacle as they enter the pew — this is a sign of reverence for the Real Presence of Christ. Follow the lead of those around you for postures: the congregation stands, sits, or kneels together. As an OCIA candidate, you may not yet receive Holy Communion — you will be dismissed after the Liturgy of the Word for further catechesis, or you may remain and participate in everything except Communion. Either is a grace-filled participation.

    Posture at Mass
    🧍
    Stand
    During the Entrance Procession, Gospel, Creed, Universal Prayer, and from the Our Father through the Communion Rite.
    🪑
    Sit
    During the First and Second Readings, the Responsorial Psalm, the Homily, the Presentation of Gifts, and after receiving Communion.

    Kneel
    From the Sanctus (Holy, Holy) through the Great Amen — the most sacred moment of the Mass when the Eucharist is consecrated.
